Coffeeshop — “Hedgehog in the Fog”
The puppet show is meant for children,
But it lands hardest
On those past forty.
Someone exhales quietly.
Someone doesn’t quite catch it.
But everyone’s running —
From something.

And the day goes on...
Like walking through a dream
with a patched-up quilt,
Where every street is haze and movement,
And every crossing asks:
“Weren’t we already here once?”

The little diner still hasn’t changed —
Same menu as ever,
Same familiar ache
That nothing ever really moves.
The old town’s a camera trap,
People lining up like pilgrims
in front of an ad
they almost believe.
And the flowers smell
like marketing.

Rain falls elsewhere.
Here — not a drop.
We pedal through the stillness,
Hunting...
The right crossing.
The right shadow.
The right stranger.

A plate of fried meat in a modest place.
A new oasis.
A boy stops his bike:
“My mom takes pictures too.
I have friends.
I don’t know my school yet.
But I live here.”

Under the overpass.
No teenagers today
eyeing your wheels.
Only fatigue.
And that feeling
you want to go home.

And you know —
the day is gone.
And you were there.
And someone,
somewhere,
is still taking a picture.
