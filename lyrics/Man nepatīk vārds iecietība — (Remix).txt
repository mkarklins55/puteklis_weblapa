I don’t like the word “tolerance” —
as if one must adapt to a breath
that isn’t theirs.
As if politeness were a torment,
and respect — a reckless compromise.

Things have gone too far —
but who drew the lines?
A little boy in a dress on the street,
and suddenly it’s the end of the world.
He didn’t shoot anyone.
He was just walking by.
But you — were afraid.

In the bar, between beer bottles, they whisper
that kissing is allowed —
but only if a glance is enough.
Their definition of freedom:
your right to say “I hate this,”
but not mine — to live.

Things have gone too far —
but who pushed them?
A father with a belt, or an actress with fear,
or maybe your unspoken urge
to dictate how love may look.

“Freedom to kill,”
says a voice on paper,
forgetting that freedom to live
is not quite the same.
No, you are not in danger —
you’re just being forced to look.
And that — you can’t bear.

“They all want things for free,”
your friend says, speaking of other skin.
Why is your pride rooted in hate?
Why does her glance make you angry?
She’s only saying —
she hurts too.

When you say “things have gone too far,”
you don’t mean facts.
You mean:
no one offers you a coat anymore,
and now you must be a person
without the privilege to lead.

Things have gone too far —
because we raised our voice.
Not by shouting,
but simply by being.
Simply by loving.
Simply walking down the street,
unhidden.

A fantastic woman mourns.
And it would be only mourning
if you didn’t see her hands
and feel the need to be amazed.
But you are amazed.
And amazement is still allowed.
