TERRIBLE WORDS
(slow-burning noir-blues, cracked baritone delivery)

[Verse 1]
Some words reek the moment they leave your tongue —
like borrowed breath that doesn’t want to go back.
“Prose” sounds like a diagnosis
spoken too proudly by someone who’s given up.
And “friend” –
it’s no longer a person,
just a sugary excuse
spilled after the sixth glass
or right before payday.

[Chorus – baritone snarl]
I’m an acquaintance –
not more, not less.
I dance for pigeons in empty courtyards,
sing to puddles and broken glass.
When you call –
I stay silent.
Because I’ve got nothing left to say.

[Verse 2 – slow bluesy monologue]
They lived in a house that leaned inward,
like thoughts that collapse mid-sentence.
We drank in silence,
every sentence like a borrowed coat
that never fit quite right.

[Bridge – muttered beat]
Once we drifted
in a boat with no oars,
no reason,
no plan.
The current took us nowhere,
and that was all we needed.
Laughter that didn’t belong,
a silence that said everything.

[Chorus – slower, raspier]
I’m an acquaintance –
just passing through.
I shuffle bottles across the table
like they’re pieces in a game
I forgot the rules to.
When they say “we got lucky,”
I light another cigarette
and tape a sign to my chest: Later.

[Verse 3 – spoken blues]
I don’t write anymore.
Don’t call.
There was a time when people interested me
just for being people.
Now I see them like furniture —
present, but not really doing anything.
And talking feels like sweating through wet cloth.

[Outro – half-laughing, mumbled]
Still, I dance.
In the mornings when no one’s watching.
A crooked step, a smirk
that cuts like a scar.
The fact that nothing excites me anymore
doesn’t mean I can’t still move.
And just because I’m quiet
doesn’t mean I’ve got no voice.
